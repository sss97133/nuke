# Make identity artwork sources

Local recognition artwork, not an affiliation claim or a vehicle-era attribution. Updated October 8, 2026.

## Sourced wordmarks and Porsche emblem

Artwork is imported from the [Cardog icons curator repository at commit 05c4001e83a0](https://github.com/cardog-ai/icons/tree/05c4001e83a0/core/optimized). Its [core license](https://github.com/cardog-ai/icons/blob/05c4001e83a0/core/LICENSE) is MIT, copyright 2024 Cardog; the complete notice is retained in [CARDOG-LICENSE.txt](./CARDOG-LICENSE.txt). These are faithful copies of the curator's supplied path artwork, not independently certified manufacturer originals. Brand names and marks remain their respective owners' trademarks.

Only each SVG root viewBox, width and height changed: fontTools SVGPath/BoundsPen computed geometry bounds, with conservative padding, to remove the source's empty 512-square canvas. All drawing paths, fills and other artwork attributes remain byte-identical. No generated lettering, installed brand font, tracing or recoloring was used. The twelve supplied Dark variants retain their supplied artwork; other monochrome assets may be inverted for dark-theme display. No SVG contains executable content, external references, font-dependent text, embedded images or an opaque rectangular background. The Porsche crest is transparent vector artwork replacing the photographic white-background JPG.

[Machine-readable provenance](./wordmarks.json) records each pinned upstream link, source/local SHA-256 digests (base64), the full upstream commit SHA-1 (base64), viewport, role and unknown era. Asset review included rasterized contact sheets on light/dark backgrounds. The Land Rover file is a lettered oval lockup, not standalone typesetting. Cardog's BMW Wordmark is a roundel and is intentionally not installed as a wordmark. Fiat's clip-path asset was not imported because this bounded path-only geometry validator did not support it. Unavailable wordmarks retain readable text; model/auction labels remain descriptive text with an optional emblem.

These assets are general brand identifiers. The upstream catalog does not establish logo validity intervals, and manufacturer incorporation/end dates are not artwork-era dates. Mixed-era populations, vehicle model years and bid calendar years do not automatically select a historical variant.

## Period lettering for text-scale rows (2026-11-08)

`vehicle-identities.json` entries with `textScale` are vector make lettering sized for a line of text (the live board
row). Each carries the dated manufacturer documents it was checked against (`evidenceDocuments`): the lettering was cut
from the period page, scaled to the same box as the rasterized SVG, binarized and scored by ink IoU; the side-by-side
overlays were inspected by eye. Year ranges stop where the evidence stops or the lettering changes. Rows render the
lettering one-colour in the text colour, as the period print forms show it; photographed badges stay headline art.

The two Commons files are byte-identical downloads (hashes in the catalogue). The Cardog Porsche wordmark is the
2024+ lettering (wider spacing after the 2023 identity refresh, and lighter strokes); it did not match the 1996-98
covers, so its former 1997 registration was removed. Not used: Porsche Design System model signatures and their Commons
re-uploads (their asset license limits use to Porsche applications); the Cardog Toyota wordmark for 1985-1992 (0.84-0.87 against
low-resolution brochures, with system-font controls at 0.81-0.83, is not proof); Commons lettering redrawn from logo aggregators
without a corroborating manufacturer source; OpenClipart drawings. No model lettering (911, Corvette, Mustang, Camaro,
Bronco, Defender, Land Cruiser) met this bar. Block-capital wordmarks are also scored against similar
system fonts set on the same crops; a range is kept only where the drawing clearly beats every font.

## 1973 Pontiac and Firebird lettering library (2026-11-11)

Several sourced versions per mark, each with its own applicability in `vehicle-identities.json`. The originals are the
unmodified upstream downloads (`sourceSha256Base64`). A headline sets each mark straight on the page, so its file is a
cutout of the original with the background removed (`*-cut.png`, `localSha256Base64`). Each entry's `cutout` names the
original, the crop in its pixels and the exact `nuke_frontend/scripts/cut-identity-lettering.cjs` command. For the
twelve cutouts made from committed originals, rerunning the command reproduces the file byte for byte. The car's own
decal starts from a `sips` PNG conversion of an uncommitted HEIC, so only its recorded hashes can be checked. Single-colour print is cut as a monochrome mark (`tone: monochrome`) and drawn as a
CSS mask in the text colour, so it follows the theme. The chrome parts keep their photographed chrome and lose the white
catalogue ground (`tone: chrome`), and the car's own decal keeps its colour (`tone: color`). `VehicleIdentity` renders the
first headline match per role: the car's own decal, then the on-car parts, then the literature versions. Entries marked
`headline: "library"` stay in the library and never set a headline. These are the boxed arrowhead, the fender bird and
the shadowed Phoenix decal photographs.

Re-cut 2026-11-11 (headline polish). The cutter gained flags, each recorded in the command it applies to:
`--min-area` drops isolated specks (the crumbs between the PONTIAC letters), `--defringe` pulls the rim in by a source
pixel, softens it and gives every edge pixel the colour of the mark beside it (no ground or paint fringe on the chrome
or the decal), and `--ground` marks the counters seen through the Firebird script (F loop, both e counters, b and d
bowls) as ground. Commands without these flags still reproduce their files byte for byte. The decal is now keyed on
green 40-90 for fuller strokes. Every cutout carries top-level `capMetrics`: the capitals' share of the file height and
the baseline's distance from its bottom, measured on a named reference glyph's columns (`glyph`, `columns`: the script's
F, the numerals' 1, mixed-case Pontiac's P, the decal's centre N and S, whole width for block capitals). Rerun with
`node nuke_frontend/scripts/cut-identity-lettering.cjs measure <file> <columns>` (or `--cap` on the cut). The headline
sets every mark at one shared cap height (0.9 of the identity's font size) on one baseline, and "-Style" text at the
same cap height. The year now leads with the US full-line cover "1973", the heaviest numeral in the library, filled in
the identity's ink; the hairline Accessorizer "1973" follows it.

The year role holds printed years only. No sourced period numeral set was found, so another year is never composed from
these digits and stays text. Trans Am lettering matches a recorded Trans Am and a Trans Am-style tribute: on a tribute it
is the lettering on the car, not factory trim, and the record's "-Style" stays visible beside it. The orange colourway
(Phoenix lists Orange 1973-75) has no sourced vendor photograph. The one car whose own photograph shows it gets its own
decal (matched by vehicle id). Any other 1973 Trans Am or tribute gets the brochure "TRANS AM" as a monochrome mark.
The car photograph is referenced by its `vehicle_images` id; its storage path carries an account id, so the path stays
out of this public repo. The original HEIC is not committed, and its SHA-256 is recorded. Product photographs show reproduction parts by GM Restoration Parts licensees, used as
reference identification with attribution. Not used: the Canadian full-size "PONTIAC '73" cover (another model line), the
1973 Firebird magazine ad (lettering too small at headline size), the later flat-red fender bird (not 1973), and a Hot
Rod road test bundled with the Canadian scans (not Pontiac literature).
