2025-11-17 12:09:38|F|Likely Causes of White Smoke After Rollover
- Oil on the heads/exhaust system  
  - During a rollover, oil can slosh into the intake, combustion chambers, or onto hot exhaust surfaces. This often burns off as white/blue smoke at idle.
- Coolant intrusion (head gasket or intake leak)  
  - White smoke can also mean coolant is entering the combustion chamber. But since everything sounds normal and this happened after a rollover, oil contamination is more likely.
- Old fuel (18 months)  
  - Gasoline degrades, loses volatility, and can cause poor combustion, fouling plugs, or smoky exhaust. It’s definitely past usable life.
- Residual fluids in intake/exhaust  
  - Oil or fuel trapped in intake runners or mufflers can burn off slowly, especially at idle.

---

✅ Immediate Steps You Should Take
- Change the oil & filter  
  - After a rollover, oil may have aerated or picked up contaminants. Fresh oil ensures you’re not circulating debris.
- Drain and replace fuel  
  - Old fuel should be completely removed. Refill with fresh super unleaded (high octane) to match the Coyote motor’s needs.
- Inspect air intake & throttle body  
  - Look for oil residue in the intake tract. Clean if necessary.
- Check spark plugs  
  - If oil fouled them, replace. Cheap insurance against misfires.
- Run engine to operating temp  
  - After fluids are replaced, let it idle and then drive gently. If smoke clears, it was residual oil. If it persists, investigate coolant leaks.
