# Proof of Work, Not Pay to Play

*Captured from Skylar, 2026-06-10 evening — mid-build, between a GitHub PAT dialog and an Xcode OAuth screen. The clearest statement of the thesis to date.*

## The statement

Nuke is **the tech layer for non-technical skilled people** — artists, builders, restorers, the people who actually put value INTO assets. We are the heavy-lifting **proof of worth** for people who create.

The incumbents got identity backwards:

- **Instagram** profits off a simple logic: *whoever gets content to us first wins.* It has the metrics and the beautiful mind-numbing content, but it's pay-to-play — people with means, money, and content teams win. It measures *presence*, not *work*.
- **LinkedIn** is a hollowed-out version of what was once a network-finding tool. It no longer knows what it is.
- **GitHub** got it right for one profession only: the contribution graph means *you can't fake the green squares*. But it only counts code.

**Nuke inverts the incumbent logic: we look at who's actually doing the work.** The barcode timeline is the green squares for people who work with their hands. A day under the Blazer is a commit. A receipt is a commit. A finished rocker panel is a merged PR. You can't buy the graph — you can only *do* it.

## Why this wins the unserved

The largest cohort of new adopters is **people who have nothing built yet** — they have nothing to lose and everything to prove, and they're looking for a system that can prove it. (Skylar, 2026-06-10 morning: "those who have nothing to lose are the largest shareholder for consumption... this system proves worth, which is the core goal — showing the value a user contributes to an asset, then watching the trajectory of that asset.")

This is "going back to how things should be — putting the control into the user." Radical data transparency as the product: your substrate, your provenance, your trajectory — visible, queryable, owned.

## Doctrine implications (binding on design decisions)

1. **The substrate is the scoreboard.** Profiles are expressions of source data; nothing on a profile may exist that the data didn't call into existence. No vanity metrics, no purchased reach.
2. **Work is the unit, not content.** Surfaces rank and render evidence of labor (days, photos-as-witness, dollars, builds) — never engagement bait.
3. **The facet tabs are a skill fingerprint** (see design-book: facet-tabs-are-a-skill-fingerprint) — automotive today; art, fashion, content production are new `kind`s in the same machine. Asset-contribution mapping generalizes: a book, a body of content, a production — all assets whose trajectory testifies to its contributors.
4. **Identity climbs the chain** (see ISSUES 2026-06-11 identity-chain doctrine): borrow Apple's tap today, become the ingestion destination tomorrow (`handle@in.nuke.ag`), and let the worth-proof profile become the identity people actually want to own.
5. **One-tap everything.** The login maze (PATs, sudo codes, 2FA webs Skylar screenshotted his way through tonight) is the enemy's UX. Ours: have a phone → tap → your work speaks.
