# Art practice in the shared contribution model: three cases against existing owners

Draft analysis for review. It is not a design decision and changes no schema, writer or data.
Read-only, from origin/main cb92be6 and the live schema on 2026-11-09 between 3:15 and 4:00 AM AST (America/St_Barthelemy, UTC-4).
One reader produced it and it is not independently verified. It follows docs/ledger/theory/data-machine-cases.md §21.4 (PR #3172), which leaves the shared model and the consumer repair open.
It adds no new tables. `party_observations` (an earlier draft) is retired as superseded.

## Ground rules applied
- Authorship, holding, documentation and rights are separate claims. Custody, an upload or a photo library never implies origin or holding.
- Museum or documentation photographs stay valuable as documentation of the depicted work and of the contributor's documenting activity. They carry no performer, artist or holding inference.
- Any identity or contribution path named below must state its source evidence and the accepted link it relies on (link type, verification method, approval state). Where no accepted link exists, the gap stays open.

## Missing existing relationships (exact)
1. **capability_fold_basis does not consume asset_observations.** `capability_fold_basis(p_actor_id)` (supabase/migrations/20261019235000_capability_fold.sql) reads component_events, work_order_resource_specifications, completion acceptances and inspection findings only. The live `actor_capability_basis.basis_kind` values are component_event, reported_performed_spec and requested_spec_conflict. No branch reads `asset_observations`, so no art claim can reach `actor_capability_basis` today.
2. **There is no accepted link from a catalogue identity to an actor (corrected).** `account_link_claims` cannot pair `actor_id` and `external_identity_id` in one row. CHECK `account_link_claims_one_target` (20261019091500_account_link_claims_actor_bridge.sql, verified live) allows either `actor_id IS NULL AND platform <> 'nuke_actor'` (the other-platform, external-identity case) or `actor_id IS NOT NULL AND external_identity_id IS NULL AND platform = 'nuke_actor' AND handle = actor_id::text` (actor-only). Actor rows are approved only at `work_evidence` / `co_sign` / `authenticated_sign` (CHECK `account_link_claims_actor_rung`). The guard trigger requires evidence for each rung: the claimant's own work_sessions row, a non-self work_order_resource_attestations row, or an observation they submitted or a session they confirmed. `viewer_identity_bridge(p_user)` (20261019093000_job_readers_authenticated.sql, verified live) and `capability_evidenced_users(p_actor_id)` read only those approved actor rows. A user can therefore hold an approved actor link and, in a separate row, an external-identity claim. No existing reader relates the two, and an art_catalogue identity (a house's printed name) is not a technician actor. Any future path must state its source evidence (for example, an approved external-identity claim with its proof_type) and the accepted actor link (method, approval state) it relies on. Neither the database nor this document matches people by name.
3. **work_sessions and technician_work_evidence are vehicle-bound and use different identity references.** `work_sessions.vehicle_id uuid NOT NULL` references vehicles; there is no asset reference; `technician_id` references `technician_phone_links`. `technician_work_evidence.technician_id` references `technicians` and `vehicle_id` references vehicles; there is no asset reference, and `technicians` has no FK to actors or users. Capabilities target `actors`. Matching names does not establish identity.
4. **The capability vocabulary is automotive.** `capability_evidence_map` (101 rows, read through capability_map_process / capability_map_operation) is automotive only: paint.refinish, welding.*, fabricate:<vehicle system>, and task_type paint maps to paint_refinish.
5. **assets.creator_id is record origin, not maker (corrected).** `COMMENT ON COLUMN public.assets.creator_id` (supabase/migrations/20261028203000_photo_work_ledger.sql:49, verified live) reads: "The person or source whose evidence created the asset row. For photo-found assets (attributes.fold_key) it is the photographer; owner_id stays NULL until the owner confirms ownership." The header of 20261109020000_held_asset_proposals.sql uses the same shape ("creator = the person whose photos found it, owner unset until they say so"). So `propose_held_assets` setting `creator_id = p_person` is correct provenance. It also carries access: `confirm_held_asset` and `dismiss_held_asset` guard on it, and `held_assets_read` lists by it. It must be preserved, never blanket-nulled.
6. **The confirmed role fault is compound confirmation and the holder projection.** `confirm_held_asset` (20261109020000_held_asset_proposals.sql) and `declare_held_asset` (20261109013000_held_assets.sql) each write, in one signature, `owner_id = signer` plus a holder_declaration AND a creation_claim (role creator, attribution direct, party_user_id = signer). That makes holding and authorship one act. Live, read 2026-11-09: 0 such creation claims exist yet. Holder projection: `asset_profile_read` sets `viewer.is_holder = (owner_id = uid OR creator_id = uid)`, and `held_assets_read` lists `owner_id = uid OR creator_id = uid` (20261109020000, lines ~170 and ~271). Record origin is therefore read as holding. `asset_observations` has no participant or role column, so roles exist only inside structured_data.

None of these choose a repair. They name which existing owner lacks which link, so the open §21.4 repair can be scoped against them.

## Source semantics notes: auction prices (doc-only, no code)
These notes are for readers that will compare art lot prices across houses. Pages were read on 2026-11-09. Each figure keeps its house's own label; nothing below backs out a hammer or decides a sale outcome.

- **Christie's: "Price realised" is hammer price plus buyer's premium.** Source: the 2025 year-end release (17 December 2025), Notes to Editors: "Estimates do not include buyer's premium. Sales totals are hammer price plus buyer's premium." (https://press.christies.com/62b-projected-global-sales-in-2025/). The per-lot figures on the two saved lot pages in PR #3176 fit the house's printed schedule, effective 1 September 2026 (https://www.christies.com/en/help/buying-guide-important-information/financial-information). The release does not say whether a guarantor's financing fee is netted out, so that is not claimed. The sale outcome (sold, unsold or price withheld) still needs source proof. Lot symbols are printed per lot (for example S02 "Third party guarantee") and are kept as printed.
- **Sotheby's: irrevocable-bid lots are reported net of the fee.** Source: the Symbol Key (https://help.sothebys.com/en/support/solutions/articles/44002518844-guide-for-buyers-symbol-key), section "⋑ Irrevocable Bids": Sotheby's "may … compensate the IBer with a fee for providing the IB, which, if they are the successful bidder, will be netted against the purchase price … The purchase price for the lot will be reported net of the fee."
  - So a Sotheby's price on a ⋑ lot that the IBer won is hammer plus premium minus an undisclosed fee, which is a different quantity from a Christie's price realised.
  - A reader must carry the ⋑ symbol with the price and must not treat the two as comparable figures. On a ⋑ lot, the fee is not stated and cannot be recovered.
- **Sotheby's: `reserveMet` is not a sale outcome.**
  - The same Symbol Key defines the reserve as "the confidential minimum hammer price at which a lot will be sold". Lots marked □ have no reserve at all.
  - A reserve-met flag only says whether bidding reached that minimum. It does not say that a sale completed: there was no withdrawal, cancellation or non-payment after the hammer, and on a □ lot there is no reserve to meet.
  - The outcome must come from the house's own sold or price statement. `reserveMet` must never be written as `result = sold`.
  - I did not re-verify this field's exact shape in the Sotheby's lot data in this pass. Treat it as a reader rule, not as proof of what the field contains.
- **Phillips: two buyer's premium schedules apply at once.** Source: "Phillips' Buyer's Premium Rates, Effective 12 April 2026" (https://dist.phillips.com/content/web/docs/forms/BuyersPremium.pdf) and Priority Bidding (https://dist.phillips.com/content/web/docs/forms/PriorityBidding.pdf).
  - The standard rates (live and timed online auctions) are 29%, 22% and 15%. The Priority Bidding rates (live auctions only) are 25%, 20% and 14%. Both use the same thresholds: New York US$2,000,000 and US$8,000,000; London GBP 1,500,000 and GBP 6,000,000; Paris €1,750,000 and €7,000,000; Hong Kong HK$15,000,000 and HK$60,000,000.
  - The lower rate applies only to a winner who placed a binding bid at or above the low estimate at least 48 hours before the sale and paid by the invoice date.
  - Watches are excluded; their New York standard rates are 27%, 21% and 14%. Local taxes are excluded.
  - Consequence: one Phillips price realised can come from two different hammers, depending on a buyer fact the lot page does not print. A reader must store both schedules with the lot and never invert a Phillips price to a hammer unless the schedule that applied is stated.
- **For any reader comparing houses:** compare like with like. Bonhams hammer, Christie's hammer plus premium (gross) and Sotheby's hammer plus premium net of the IB fee on ⋑ lots are three different quantities. Phillips adds a buyer-dependent premium on top. Keep each in its own house's label and add no cross-house price derivation until each basis is stated per lot.
