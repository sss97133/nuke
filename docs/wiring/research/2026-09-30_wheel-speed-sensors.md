# Wheel speed sensors, tone rings and knuckle mounts (2026-09-30)

Owner, 2026-09-29, voice-typed: "we need to deal with TPS sensors and then the other side of the sensor which we have to
figure out how to install that machine or whatever machining part of the axle the knuckle worth looking into what do we do
there".

Read as wheel speed (ABS) sensors: "the other side of the sensor" is the tone ring the sensor reads, and "machining part of the
axle, the knuckle" is how a ring and a sensor mount get fitted to axles that never had them. Tire-pressure (TPMS) sensors sit
inside the wheel and need no axle or knuckle work, so they are not covered.

Prepared 2026-09-29 by the wheel-speed lane. Web pages were read on 2026-09-29 and saved as markdown in
`reference_documents/web_snapshots/<file>` (gitignored; a third party's text never goes into this public repo). Forum posts
are marked as forum posts: one person's report, not a maker's specification. Nothing here is bought, sent or decided.

**Verdict.**
- Nothing on this truck needs wheel speeds to run, stop, shift or show speed.
- Wheel speeds buy three things:
  - traction control in 2WD;
  - launch control in 2WD;
  - slip logging.
- They add nothing to the brakes until an ABS is fitted, and an ABS would bring its own sensors.
- They are worth fitting only if Skylar wants traction or launch control.
  - **The price in harness capacity:** 2 of the 61-pin's last 3 spare cavities (d, t, u), the M130's last two universal
    digital inputs (B10, B11; B11 is also what the NAV-L10 GPS alternate wants), and one PDM15 output.
  - That is a big ask for an optional feature.
- If he does, two front sensors are MoTeC's minimum: one driven wheel and one non-driven wheel. The NP205 sender (#100 on M130
  B08/UDIG3) already measures the driven rear. MoTeC's own GPR example M130 pinout names B08 UDIG3 "Wheel Speed Rear Drive
  Sensor" (GPR page, M130 connector B table), which is exactly the job #100 does now.

**Pick: ZF GS100502.** Why:
- **Supply.** It runs on 5–24 V and on an unregulated supply ("Compatible with unregulated power supply", datasheet p.1).
- **Input match.** Its open-collector output suits both the M130 UDIG (switchable 3k3 pull-up to 5 V) and an E888 digital input
  (2700 Ω pull-up), per M1 hardware techspec p.9 and E888/E816 manual Appendix B p.17.
- **Gap.** The M12 thread sets the air gap with two jam nuts and a feeler gauge, so there is no shim stack.
- **Lead.** The 1 m lead reaches from the knuckle to a connector on the frame.
- **Target.** ZF publishes a target geometry to design the ring against.
- **Availability.** It is in stock at a published price.

**Fallback for heat: Bosch HA-M** (rated 160 °C against the ZF's 125 °C), once the corner temperature is measured (§3.4).

- **ZF pull-up.** ZF recommends a 1 k pull-up at 5 V. The M130's internal pull-up is 3k3, so its edges are softer at high
  frequency. Wheel frequencies stay under 1 kHz (§3.1 table), well below the sensor's 15 kHz, but the scope check in §4.3 is
  still required.

## 3. Tone rings, each corner

### 3.1 Front: Dana 44, 1977 K10 hub-and-disc

**What is there.**
- **Hub and rotor.** The 1977 K10/K20 front hub and rotor come off together as the "hub-and-disc assembly". It runs on
  serviceable tapered bearings, set to ".001 to .010 inch end play" (1977 LTSM pp.305–306). Whether the disc is cast with the
  hub or bolted to it is `{unknown, needs: look at the part}`; a bolted disc would allow a ring clamped between hub and disc
  instead of a pressed one.
- **Spindle.** The spindle bolts to the knuckle; "Torque spindle nuts to 25 ft. lbs. (K10, 20)", under the section's fastener
  CAUTION (LTSM p.307).
- **Hubs.** Part-time trucks have free-wheeling hubs (LTSM p.305).
- **Parts in the records.**
  - 2021-05 receipt lines, as written: "Bearing BGS A35", "Bearing BGS LM104949", "Oil seal SLS 471271", "Brake Rotor
    (Bendix) BDX TN145514" (`receipt_items`, K5 receipts).
  - Offroad Design crossover steering on the passenger knuckle, 2024-09-12 (`receipt_items`: GK39GMD442-3R, U9010-CR, U9011-R
    "aftermarket 4-stud knuckle").
- **Photos.**
  - 2021-05-08: old hub, new rotor and ACDelco caliper (photos 44bb12d5, 57e13a10).
  - 2023-11-27: freshly painted Dana 44 with the crossover arm (photo c3c86958).
  - 2024-09-21: rebuilt front axle (photo 0718ba62).

**No GM part to borrow.** Our 1977 and 1987 GM truck manuals have no antilock section: library_search "antilock" and
"anti-lock" find only RPO-code rows in `GM_RPO_Master_List.pdf` (pp.99–101, 160). No catalogued ring for this hub-and-disc
was found. MoTeC's guidance for a vehicle that never had sensors: "have them made to suit" (Launch guide p.4).

**The design** (to be priced by the machine shop):
- **Where.** A steel ring pressed onto a turned register on the inboard barrel of each hub-and-disc: behind the rotor, over the
  inner-bearing bore. The sensor points radially at the teeth from a bracket on the knuckle (§4).
- **Why there.**
  - The ring turns with the wheel whether the free-wheeling hub is locked or not.
  - The barrel carries the bearing cups, so a register turned true to the cups holds the runout.
- **Material.** Low-carbon cold-rolled steel, per ZF's recommendation (datasheet p.2). The grade (for example 1018) and the
  anti-rust finish are the shop's call.
- **Fit.** Interference fit on the turned register. The interference value is the shop's call against the ring's section.
- **Teeth.** At least ZF's minimum geometry: 5.0 mm high, 2.5 mm wide, 10 mm between teeth, 6.35 mm thick. That is a 12.5 mm
  pitch.
- **Tooth count.** N = floor(π × tooth-tip diameter ÷ 12.5 mm). MoTeC wants at least 24, and 48 or more is preferred.

| Ring tip diameter | Teeth at ZF's minimum pitch |
|---|---|
| 100 mm | 25 |
| 110 mm | 27 |
| 120 mm | 30 |
| 130 mm | 32 |
| 150 mm | 37 |
| 191 mm | 48 |

- **Minimum sizes.** 24 teeth needs a 95.5 mm tip diameter; 48 teeth needs 191 mm, too large for the space behind a rotor.
- **Finer pitch.** A finer pitch that gives more teeth is allowed only after a bench test ("smaller dimensions may work, but
  testing for the application is required", ZF p.2).
- **Hub barrel OD.** Unknown. It sets the ring size and the tooth count: `{unknown, needs: measure the inboard barrel OD and
  its free length on a hub-and-disc}`.

**Signal frequency.** f = N × v ÷ (π × tire diameter). The worked example uses an 800 mm tire; the truck's tire size is
unknown.

| Teeth | 1 km/h | 20 km/h | 160 km/h |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | 2.7 Hz | 53.1 Hz | 424 Hz |
| 27 | 3.0 Hz | 59.7 Hz | 478 Hz |
| 48 | 5.3 Hz | 106.1 Hz | 849 Hz |

- **Receiving inputs.** All of these are inside the ZF's 15 kHz and the E888's 1 Hz–6 kHz digital-input range
  (Appendix B p.17).
- **Lowest speed.** At the E888's 1 Hz floor, 27 teeth read down to 0.34 km/h.

### 3.2 Rear: GM semi-floating axle with the bolt-on disc conversion

**Which axle.**
- **Axle type.** No record names it. The 1977 LTSM lists the half-ton semi-floating axles as the "8-1/2" and 8-7/8" Ring Gear
  Axle", with axle shafts held by C-locks in the differential (LTSM p.354, pp.365–366).
- **Cover bolts.** The installed aftermarket finned cover (photo 5aefc0bd, 2023-11-28) shows 11 cover bolts in view. A 10-bolt
  cover has 10, so this points to the 12-bolt 8-7/8". `{unknown, needs: count the cover bolts (12) or read the housing casting}`.
  It matters only for WSS-4E.
- **Brakes.** Disc conversion: a vented hat rotor over the axle flange, a GM-style caliper with a parking-brake lever, and a
  plate bracket with a welded doubler bolted to the tube-end brake flange (photos c475159e and 23ffbfd5, 2023-11-27). The kit is
  logged as "Rear Disc brake kit" in the 2024-08-29 build log (work record 6aa3b1de).

**Recommended path: no rear sensors.**
- The NP205 sender already gives the rear drive speed.
- MoTeC's minimum is one driven plus one non-driven sensor.
- MoTeC's own M130 example wires the rear drive speed to B08 UDIG3.

**If four corners are wanted (WSS-4E):**
- **Ring under the stud heads (R-b).** A thin toothed ring clamped between the axle flange's inboard face and the wheel-stud
  heads, read radially from a tab on the existing caliper bracket plate.
  - It changes the stud grip, so it may need longer studs. Shop to confirm.
- **Ring on the shaft (R-c).** A ring pressed onto the axle shaft inboard of the flange.
  - The shafts come out through the differential (C-locks, LTSM pp.365–366).
  - The land diameter must be measured.
- **Rejected.**
  - A ring sandwiched between flange and rotor: it moves the rotor off the caliper.
  - A single sensor in the differential (the RWAL-style layout): it reads only the rear average, which the NP205 sender already
    gives.

## 4. The mount on the knuckle

### 4.1 Bracket

- **What it is.** A bolt-on steel plate, one per side, mirrored.
- **Where it bolts.** Two candidate points:
  1. Under two of the spindle nuts. Stock nuts are 25 ft-lb (K10/K20, LTSM p.307), and the fastener CAUTION applies: keep GM's
     grade, full thread engagement and torque with the plate's thickness added.
  2. Off the bolts of the caliper support bracket ("the two mounting bolts which attach the caliper to the support bracket",
     1977 LTSM p.411). Its torque is `{unknown, needs: the 1977 LTSM section 5 caliper page read from the page image}`. The
     65 ft-lb anchor-plate figure in `output/K5_dimensions_atoms.yaml` comes from the Mitchell sheet that file is known to
     misread (receipt 2026-09-30_research-vehicle-geometry.md), so it is not used.
  - The shop picks whichever gives a clear radial line to the ring, clear of the caliper, the dust shield and the crossover
    steering arm.
- **Nothing is welded to the knuckle or the spindle** (agent design rule: they are steering parts). A drilled and tapped boss is
  the alternative, made with the knuckle off.
- **Plate thickness.** The shop's stiffness call. Its natural frequency must stay clear of vibration; the Bosch HA-M is rated to
  1,200 m/s² at 10 Hz–2 kHz (HA-M page) and the ZF to 15 g at 40 Hz–2 kHz (datasheet p.1).
- **Setting the gap.** The ZF threads through an M12×1 tapped hole (or a clearance hole with two M12×1 jam nuts).
  1. Turn the ring to its runout high point.
  2. Set 1.5 mm with a feeler gauge.
  3. Lock the jam nuts at no more than 5.65 N·m.
  4. Mark the nuts.
  - The Littelfuse and Bosch fallbacks set the gap with a shim stack under the flange instead.
- **Strain relief.** A cushion-clamp hole on the plate for the sensor lead, close to the sensor. The spacing is the builder's
  call (§5.2).

### 4.2 Steering and axle travel

- **Steering.** The bracket steers with the knuckle, so the air gap does not change through the steering range.
- **What moves is the lead.** It spans knuckle to frame through full lock both ways and full axle travel.
- **The factory solution to that span is the brake hose.** It runs from the frame bracket (U-clip) to the caliper (1977 LTSM
  p.398), and it must never be twisted (pp.414, 417).
- **Route the sensor lead with the hose** and fix the DTM connector at the frame end, inside the rail channel (§5.2), never
  on the moving knuckle.
- **Steering lock angle, axle travel and hose free length are unknown:** `{unknown, needs: measure the hose's free length and
  the bracket positions; cycle lock to lock at full droop and full bump with the lead taped in place; builder [HANDS]}`.
- **Clearance.** Keep 0.75 in from moving parts. That is GM's rule for brake pipes (LTSM p.398), used here as the benchmark.
  Watch the crossover drag link across the passenger knuckle top.

## 5. Wiring

### 5.1 Cable

- **Shielding.**
  - The canon's shielded list is crank, cam, knock, CAN, wideband and video. Wheel speed is not on it (ch.16 §7.1).
  - Bosch ships its HA-M Hall sensor on an AWG 24 lead in DR-25, with no shield listed (HA-M page, Connectors and Wires).
  - This build's pattern for three-wire Hall sensors is crank and cam: M27500 2-conductor shielded (signal + 0 V), a separate
    supply wire, and the drain at the ECU end only (registry #99, #99r, #99s; K5_connector_schedule.txt:38 via the registry
    note).
- **Contacts decide the gauge.** Deutsch DTM size-20 solid contacts 0460-202-20141 / 0462-201-20141 take 20 AWG only
  (`catalog/parts.yaml`, Deutsch contacts catalog p.125). So the corner-to-ECU conductors are 20 AWG M22759/32 (the §1
  12–20 AWG lock), with each sensor's signal twisted with its 0 V.
- **Default: unshielded.** A shield is Dave's call. A drain carried to the M130 costs one more 61-pin cavity; M27500 20 AWG
  shielded cable is not picked (state 0f(b)).

### 5.2 Route and clips

- **The routing authority is `objectTraits.ts`.**
  - Wheel wells are a "Forbidden zone" ("wires never route through wheel wells (rocks, water, salt)"; a brake-wear lead
    "routes via inner fender, NOT through the wheel well").
  - The frame rail is the canonical path: `channel_along: true`, "Inside the C-channel", clipped "every 12\"";
    `channel_over: false` (road debris).
  - Source: `nuke_frontend/src/components/wiring/objectTraits.ts` (Wheel_*, Under_Frame_Blazer).
- **Knuckle to frame.** The one span a wheel sensor cannot avoid, so keep it short and armoured.
  - The ZF 1 m lead, sleeved in DR-25 (the build's outer jacket, canon ch.16 §6), runs along the front brake hose to the frame.
  - The DTM connector clips inside the frame rail's C-channel beside the hose bracket, not in the open wheel well.
  - The rear, in WSS-4E only, runs along the axle tube with the steel brake pipes, to the axle junction block, then up the rear
    hose to the frame (LTSM p.398).
- **Frame to engine bay.** Inside the frame rail channel (objectTraits above; frame doctrine,
  `research/2026-06-10_power_spine_builders_study.md` §1.2). Rubber-lined cushion clamps (MS21919 family, spine study §1.3):
  - every 12 in on the frame rail (objectTraits);
  - never more than 18 in (455 mm) anywhere (ABYC E-11, via the spine study);
  - where the lead joins a bundle, ties every 150 mm on the trunk and 75 mm on branches (IPC/WHMA-A-620 via appendix-g:304,
    canon ch.16 §8.5).
- **Clearances.**
  - 0.75 in from anything that moves (GM's brake-pipe rule, LTSM p.398, used as the benchmark).
  - 1 in plus DR-25 near the headers (objectTraits engine note, via spine study §1.4).

### 5.3 Axle-to-frame flex loop

- The loop between the last fixed clamp on the frame and the first clamp on the knuckle rides with the hose.
- **Its free length is at least the hose's free length** (agent design rule; the hose is GM's proven span for this motion).
- Proven by the lock-to-lock and droop-to-bump cycle in §4.2 [HANDS].

- **Why the supply and ground stay in the engine bay:**
  - A short at a wheel corner stays on a PDM15 output with its own current limit.
  - It never pulls down the M130's 5 V or 6.3 V sensor rails (crank, cam, pedal and TPS ride on those).
  - Only the two signals cross the firewall.
- **Offset trade-off:** the signals are then read against a ground in another place. An open-collector low is ≤ 0.6 V at 20 mA
  (Littelfuse; the ZF sinks 20 mA max), and the UDIG threshold is programmable, so a small offset between ground banks is
  tolerable.
- **The MoTeC-pattern alternative:** supply from SEN_5V0_B (A9), 0 V to SEN_0V. It costs two more 61-pin cavities and couples
  corner faults to the ECU rails. MoTeC warns "no current paths" should route to sensor 0 V pins (techspec p.14).
- **Dave's call** between the two.

## 6. Recommendation and the candidates (catalog/options.yaml)

**Recommended: WSS-F2, two front wheel speed sensors into the M130.** Why:
- **Needs.** It meets MoTeC's minimum for launch and traction control (one non-driven + one driven), because the NP205 sender
  (#100) is the rear drive speed MoTeC's own example puts on B08.
- **Documented path.** It uses the input MoTeC documents for wheel speed (UDIG), so there is no expander and no
  CAN-mapping unknown.
- **Machining.** Only the two front hub-and-discs are machined; the rear is untouched.
- **Cost.**
  - Two ZF sensors at $33.80 (DigiKey 2026-09-29).
  - Two DTM pairs.
  - The machine shop (quote).
- **Capacity.** Two M130 pins, two 61-pin cavities and one PDM15 output.

- **Conflict.** `conflicts_with: [NAV-L10]`: both name M130 B11 (UDIG6).

**Kept as toggles:**
- **WSS-4E** (`alternative_to: WSS-F2`): four corners into a cab-mounted E888.
  - Four-wheel slip.
  - The fronts cross the 61-pin the same way; the rears enter at the small rear bulkhead the owner allows (state 0ah).
  - The E888's GPR mapping and price are open, and the rear rings need shaft or stud work.
- **WSS-PREP** (`alternative_to: WSS-F2`): rings and brackets only, no sensors or wires. The long-lead machining gets done
  while the hubs are easy to pull; wires come later if the 61-pin can spare the cavities.
